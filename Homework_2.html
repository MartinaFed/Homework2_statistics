4. Academic Discussion & Limitations

Similarities & Differences: Both are cryptographic schemes designed to obscure data. However, the Caesar cipher operates symmetrically on individual characters, shifting them within a closed alphabet ($\vert{}\mathcal{K}\vert{} = 26$). The ECC scheme is asymmetric and maps values over an algebraic group defined by a Weierstrass equation. ECC destroys linguistic statistics, replacing them with a uniform pseudo-random byte stream.

The Statistical Attack (Caesar): The Chi-square test computes the divergence between the letter counts in the ciphertext and the expected distribution of the English language. As demonstrated by the graph above, Limitation: if the ciphertext is too short (under 15-20 letters), the statistical distribution hasn't normalized, causing false positives. At larger lengths, success converges to 100%.

The Algebraic Attack (Toy ECC): This attack completely ignores the ciphertext and targets the mathematical relationship $Q = d \cdot G$. Because the key space was restricted, a linear $\mathcal{O}(N)$ search finds the scalar $d$.

Why Real ECC is Secure: In a properly configured secp256k1 scheme, $d$ is a 256-bit integer. Even utilizing advanced $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$ algorithms, it would require $\approx 2^{128}$ operations. This renders the algebraic brute-force approach computationally impossible. The flaw here is purely the restricted key space, not the ECC geometry itself.
